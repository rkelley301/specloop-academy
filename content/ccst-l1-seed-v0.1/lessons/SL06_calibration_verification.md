# SL06 — Calibration versus verification
> **DRAFT — TECHNICAL REVIEW REQUIRED.** This lesson is original educational draft content and has not been technically validated. Do not publish, sell, or use it as approved training until a qualified instrumentation and controls reviewer verifies the technical details, answer key, and safety language.

**Objective:** Distinguish checking an instrument from adjusting it and explain why as-found results matter.

## Audio script

Verification asks whether an instrument performs within an accepted tolerance under stated test conditions. Calibration is the comparison of an instrument’s response with a known reference. Depending on the result and procedure, calibration work may include adjustment.

[PAUSE 3 SECONDS]

A useful record separates the as-found condition from the as-left condition. As-found means what the instrument did before adjustment. As-left means what it did after any approved adjustment. If you record only the final result, you lose evidence about the condition discovered in the field.

[PAUSE 3 SECONDS]

The reference used for comparison should be suitable for the task and handled under the applicable quality process. Record the range, test points, readings, tolerance or acceptance criterion, equipment identification, and result as required by the procedure.

[PAUSE 3 SECONDS]

Do not adjust an instrument simply because a value differs from expectation. First verify the correct range, procedure, reference, environmental conditions, and measurement setup. Then follow the approved work instruction and authorization.

[PAUSE 3 SECONDS]

The practical memory aid is: verify means determine whether it meets criteria; calibrate means compare with a reference and, when authorized, adjust. Preserve both the condition found and the condition left.

## Recall check
1. What does “as-found” describe?
2. Why should a record include as-left results too?
3. Does every calibration comparison automatically authorize adjustment?

## Scenario
A device is found outside tolerance, adjusted, and then passes. What two states should the record preserve, and why?

## Answer key
1. The measured performance before adjustment.
2. It documents the final condition after work and supports traceability.
3. No. Follow the applicable procedure and authorization.

**Scenario answer:** As-found and as-left. The as-found result shows the condition discovered; the as-left result shows the condition after the authorized action.
